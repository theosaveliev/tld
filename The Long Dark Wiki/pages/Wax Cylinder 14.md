## Transcript

_The following text is transcribed from **Wax Cylinder 14**_

Erik, I feel alone here.

Yes, I have my students, and they are sweethearts.

But apart from the Reverend, I have not made many friends here.

The Reverend -- he insists I use his Christian name, Angus, but I find it uncomfortable as this is not how things are done back home in Norway.

He has been generous with his time, checking in on me.

Last week he left me one of McLeod's cylinder players, and some music to listen to. He said he felt it would help me remain in a "pastoral" mood.

And indeed, it has helped.

But I find myself thinking more and more about you.

I know you are suffering, and if not for the money I make here I would see no reason for us to be half a world apart.

I heard from your mother that she had you out at the seaside, and that the air did you good.

She said your lungs are still hurting. Why did you tell me otherwise in your last letter?

My plan is, every time I feel lonely for home, to record a message to you and tell you something beautiful I have seen.

And I will record it in English, so that you are forced to practice! This way, when you are better, we can travel abroad together.

I love Norway, but if there is one thing this experience has taught me, it's that there is a whole big world out there for us to discover.

We cannot just hide at home, where we feel safe.

Now for a beautiful thing.

This morning I watched the sun rise over the rocky landscape. As the warmth hit the water, a mist began to rise, and the light made the mist glow like candlelight.

It reminded me of our small church back home, and the way the candles flicker over your face, whenever you play the organ there.

I miss home. I miss you, Erik.

I will send money with this message.

All my love.

Your Elsa.
